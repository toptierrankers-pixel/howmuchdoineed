Your measurements

01How to calculate gravel

Gravel is sold by volume (cubic yards) or by weight (tons). Both start from the same measurement: length × width × depth. Because depth is usually given in inches, you divide by 12 to convert it to feet before multiplying.

cubic feet = length (ft) × width (ft) × depth (in) ÷ 12 cubic yards = cubic feet ÷ 27 tons ≈ cubic yards × 1.4

The 1.4 figure is the rough density of common gravel, around 1.4 tons per cubic yard. Different products (pea gravel, crushed stone, road base) run slightly heavier or lighter, so confirm the exact density with your supplier before ordering by weight.

02Worked example

A 20 ft × 10 ft path, 3 inches deep

  1. Volume in cubic feet: 20 × 10 × (3 ÷ 12) = 50 ft³
  2. Convert to cubic yards: 50 ÷ 27 = 1.85 yd³
  3. Add a 10% waste factor: 1.85 × 1.10 = 2.04 yd³
  4. Convert to tons: 2.04 × 1.4 = 2.85 tons
Order about 2 cubic yards (≈ 2.9 tons) of gravel.

04Common questions

How many tons are in a cubic yard of gravel?

Roughly 1.4 tons for typical gravel, though it ranges from about 1.4 to 1.5 tons depending on the stone type, size, and how wet it is. Crushed stone tends to sit at the heavier end.

How deep should gravel be?

Two inches is enough for a light path or decorative bed. A driveway needs more. Plan on 4 inches for a top layer over an existing base, or up to 6 inches if you're building the base from scratch.

Why add a waste factor?

Ground is never perfectly flat, gravel compacts as it settles, and some is always lost in handling. Adding 5-10% means you won't run short partway through the job. The calculator applies this for you.

Should I order by volume or weight?

Suppliers may quote either. Cubic yards describe the space to fill; tons describe what gets loaded on the truck. Use the tonnage figure to order, but keep the cubic-yard figure to check the delivery looks right.
