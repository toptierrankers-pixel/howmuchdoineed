Slab dimensions

01How to calculate concrete

Concrete volume is length × width × thickness. Thickness is entered in inches, so it's divided by 12 to convert to feet, then the total is divided by 27 to reach cubic yards, the unit ready-mix trucks use.

cubic feet = length (ft) × width (ft) × thickness (in) ÷ 12 cubic yards = cubic feet ÷ 27 60 lb bags = cubic feet ÷ 0.45 80 lb bags = cubic feet ÷ 0.60

A 60 lb bag of mix yields about 0.45 cubic feet of set concrete; an 80 lb bag yields about 0.60. Bagged mixing only makes sense for small jobs. Once you're past roughly half a cubic yard, ready-mix is cheaper and far less work.

02Worked example

A 10 ft × 10 ft slab, 4 inches thick

  1. Volume in cubic feet: 10 × 10 × (4 ÷ 12) = 33.3 ft³
  2. Convert to cubic yards: 33.3 ÷ 27 = 1.23 yd³
  3. Add a 10% waste factor: 1.23 × 1.10 = 1.36 yd³
  4. In 80 lb bags: 36.7 ÷ 0.60 = 62 bags
Order about 1.4 cubic yards of ready-mix. Bagging this by hand (62 bags) isn't worth it.

04Common questions

How many 80 lb bags make a cubic yard?

About 45 bags, since each yields roughly 0.60 cubic feet and a cubic yard is 27 cubic feet. That's a lot of mixing. It's the point where ready-mix delivery wins.

How thick should a concrete slab be?

Four inches suits patios, walkways, and shed bases. Step up to 5-6 inches for driveways or any surface that takes vehicle loads. Footings and structural work follow their own specs.

Should I order extra?

Yes. Forms flex, subgrade isn't perfectly level, and you can't pause a pour to buy more. A 10% cushion is standard, which this calculator includes.

Bags or ready-mix?

Bags win for posts and small pads under about half a yard. Beyond that, ready-mix is cheaper per yard and saves hours of mixing, plus you get one continuous pour with no cold joints.
