Paint Calculator

Work out how many gallons of paint to buy. Enter the wall area you're covering, the number of coats, and the coverage on the can to get a gallon count rounded up for purchase.

Project details

01How to calculate paint

Paint is estimated from the area to cover, multiplied by the number of coats, divided by the coverage rate printed on the can, usually around 350 square feet per gallon.

wall area = perimeter (ft) × height (ft) [minus doors & windows] gallons = wall area × coats ÷ coverage (sq ft/gal)

If you don't know your wall area, measure the total length around the room and multiply by the wall height, then subtract about 20 square feet for each door and 15 for each window. Enter that figure as your area.

02Worked example

A room with 400 sq ft of wall, 2 coats

  1. Area × coats: 400 × 2 = 800 sq ft to cover
  2. Divide by coverage: 800 ÷ 350 = 2.29 gallons
  3. Round up to buy: 3 gallons
Buy 3 gallons. The extra covers touch-ups and the odd thin spot.

04Common questions

How much does a gallon of paint cover?

About 350 square feet per coat on smooth walls. Rough or textured surfaces drink up more, dropping to 250-300, so use the coverage figure on your specific can.

How many coats do I need?

Two is standard for good coverage and an even finish. One coat can work when repainting a similar color. Big color changes may need two coats over primer.

Do I subtract doors and windows?

For a tighter estimate, yes, roughly 20 sq ft per door and 15 per window. For a small room it barely changes the gallon count, so it's optional.

Should I buy extra?

Keep a little on hand for touch-ups, and rounding up to the next whole gallon usually covers that. Same batch means a perfect color match later.
